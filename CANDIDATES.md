# Candidates — Charm the Commuter: Forecast a City That Isn't

Six one-line forecasts, each for a city that does not exist, each scored
against the judge's five criteria (most important first):

1. Reads as a one-line forecast for a city that does not exist (city named,
   weather the whole point)
2. Makes a bored commuter smile on one reading (small joke, surprising
   image, turn of phrase)
3. Keeps the register of a real forecast (broadcast-weather rhythm, bent not
   dropped)
4. One concrete detail of the city's weather or life, so it feels like a place
5. Uses its 140 characters well

## 1. Kettlewick — 120 characters

> Kettlewick: rain falls upward today, 14°C, so Mara's umbrella opens over
> her shoes while the bus queue applauds the sky.

- (1) Strong — city named first, rain is the point.
- (2) Good — umbrella over her shoes is a funny image; "applauds the sky"
  lands.
- (3) Good — "rain falls upward today, 14°C" is forecast-shaped.
- (4) Good — the bus queue, the umbrella.
- (5) Good — every word earns its place, though "applauds the sky" is
  slightly abstract.
- Verdict: strong, but the joke is a little broad.

## 2. Brumefall — 131 characters

> In Brumefall, drizzle tastes of peppermint at 6°C, and old Tomas still
> irons his newspaper dry on the radiator before the 7:40 bus.

- (1) Strong — city named, drizzle and temperature lead.
- (2) Strong — "irons his newspaper dry" is an odd, smile-worthy image.
- (3) Good — temperature + time reads like a morning forecast.
- (4) Strong — the radiator, the 7:40 bus, the newspaper.
- (5) Good — dense, no wasted words.
- Verdict: very strong; the peppermint drizzle is the most surprising
  sensory detail of the six, but the scene is Tomas at home, not the
  commuter at the stop.

## 3. Zorblat — 118 characters

> Zorblat: fog so thick the 8:15 bus arrives before it leaves; Lina waves
> at her own reflection, which waves back, late.

- (1) Strong — city named, fog is the point.
- (2) Strong — the bus arriving before it leaves is a neat paradox.
- (3) Fair — reads more like a riddle than a forecast; no temperature.
- (4) Good — the 8:15 bus, the reflection.
- (5) Good — tight.
- Verdict: charming, but the forecast register (3) is the weakest of the six.

## 4. Puddlewick — 129 characters

> Tonight in Puddlewick, rain taps Morse code on tin roofs — three dots for
> 'stay' — and the night bus answers with its headlights.

- (1) Good — city named, rain is the point, though "tonight" shifts it
  away from the morning commute.
- (2) Strong — Morse-code rain is a lovely surprising image.
- (3) Good — "tonight" + rain reads like an evening forecast.
- (4) Strong — tin roofs, Morse code, headlights.
- (5) Good — every word works.
- Verdict: beautiful, but no named person and no commuter in the scene;
  it is a mood, not a moment with someone in it.

## 5. Glimmerhollow — 121 characters ★ CHOSEN

> Glimmerhollow: snow falls in slow motion at 2°C, so Eda catches one
> flake, frames it, and misses three buses admiring it.

- (1) Strong — city named first, snow and temperature are the point.
- (2) Strong — framing a single snowflake while missing three buses is
  funny and warm.
- (3) Strong — "snow falls in slow motion at 2°C" is pure broadcast
  weather, bent by one impossible word.
- (4) Strong — the framed flake, the three missed buses.
- (5) Strong — 121 of 140 characters, nothing wasted.
- Verdict: the best of the six. A concrete scene (named person, concrete
  object, one surprising detail), it ends on an image, it speaks directly
  to the bored commuter at the bus stop, and it never moralises.

## 6. Vesper-on-Sea — 119 characters

> In Vesper-on-Sea, the wind steals hats at 40 km/h and returns them by
> post; today Jonah's cap arrives before it leaves.

- (1) Strong — city named, wind speed is forecast-shaped.
- (2) Good — hats returned by post is a delightful idea.
- (3) Strong — wind speed in km/h is classic forecast register.
- (4) Good — the posted cap.
- (5) Good — tight.
- Verdict: strong, but the twist ("arrives before it leaves") is a time
  paradox like candidate 3, and it is one twist short of candidate 5's
  full scene.

## Chosen entry

Candidate 5, Glimmerhollow (121 characters), is the entry written by
